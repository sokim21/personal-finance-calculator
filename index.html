Retirement inputs

Work backwards from your desired spending, as in Lecture 7B.

Enter balances and annual contributions earmarked for retirement. Use the retirement tax field for an estimate of taxes on withdrawals. Account types do not drive withdrawal order or tax calculations.

Cash uses its own return before retirement. The model pools retirement assets at retirement and applies the selected portfolio return.

Property and other assets

Add homes, land, rental or commercial buildings, and business interests separately from investment balances. Enter whole-property amounts before applying your ownership share. Keep property costs entered here out of base spending.

Annual spending in today’s dollars

Enter gross benefits. Include healthcare premiums, including any deducted from benefits, and total annual taxes in the separate fields. Each spending input grows with general inflation.

Social Security and pension

Use your own worker benefit, in today’s dollars. Check whether your SSA estimate assumes earnings after you plan to stop working. For a partial benefit under an international agreement, enter the estimate SSA gives you at the chosen age.

Started working in the U.S. later?

Regular eligibility generally takes 40 credits. The benefit uses your highest 35 years of indexed U.S. covered earnings, with zeros for missing years. Some international agreements can help you qualify for a partial benefit. Under the U.S.–South Korea agreement, you generally need at least six U.S. credits to use Korean credits for U.S. eligibility. Enter a foreign pension separately in the fields below.

Amounts are in today’s dollars. Each benefit’s inflation setting applies from today through its start age and during payment years.

Foreign pension or foreign public benefit

Enter the amount in U.S. dollars. The model uses the exchange rate reflected in your input.
